\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We present automatic neural baking, a representation that composes directly with the traditional
rendering pipeline: an element-wise ``texture'' whose domain is the mesh surface and whose blending
is order-independent, so the asset needs no UV atlas and no depth sort and is evaluated per fragment
at real-time rates. A 2D Gaussian mixture anchored in barycentric coordinates carries the
view-independent fields, an adaptive hierarchical environment map the incident light, and a FastKAN
decoder the view-dependent term. With this bridge in place, the next steps are engine
integration, real captures, and element-level operations such as merging or editing disks directly.

\paragraph{Scope.} Our method takes the mesh as input and is optimized per scene, and we evaluate on
synthetic single-object benchmarks. Within a near-convex part, light arriving from the rest of the
object is treated as distant and position-independent, so strongly concave objects are the hardest
case: \emph{toaster} has our lowest absolute PSNR, even though we still lead every context method
there (Table~\ref{tab:shiny_perscene}). Real-time evaluation is demonstrated in
nvdiffrast rather than a shipped shader. We evaluate with the provided meshes; behaviour on
reconstructed meshes is not studied here. Because the anchoring is linear in the vertices,
appearance gradients also reach the mesh; App.~\ref{app:refinement} uses this to refine a
reconstructed mesh, and a full study of refinement is left to future work.
