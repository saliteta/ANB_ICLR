\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We present automatic neural baking, which optimizes view-dependent appearance as an element-wise
``texture'' whose domain is the mesh surface and whose blending is order-independent --- so the
asset needs no UV atlas and no depth sort, and is evaluated per fragment at real-time rates. Three
interchangeable modules realize it: a 2D Gaussian mixture anchored in barycentric coordinates for
the view-independent fields, an adaptive hierarchical environment map for far-field and near-field
reflection, and a FastKAN decoder for the view-dependent term. Given the same geometry, the result
surpasses prior work on both benchmarks. Because the field is anchored to the faces, the same
optimization also refines the mesh it is baked onto, and the trained mixture reduces to a compact
asset without retraining.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Our method requires a mesh, and is optimized per scene. We evaluate on
synthetic single-object benchmarks; real captures, where the input mesh is noisier and the
illumination less controlled, remain untested. Within a near-convex part, light arriving from the
rest of the object is treated as distant and position-independent, so strongly concave objects are
the hardest case --- \emph{toaster} is where our margin is smallest. Real-time evaluation is
demonstrated in nvdiffrast rather than in a shipped fragment shader; the representation is
shader-implementable by construction, but engine integration is left to future work. Mesh refinement
adjusts an existing surface and cannot recover geometry that is absent, and is validated with a
single environment map.
