\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We present automatic neural baking, a representation that lets appearance compose directly with
the traditional rendering pipeline: an element-wise ``texture'' whose domain is the mesh surface
and whose blending is order-independent, so the asset needs no UV atlas and no depth sort, and is
evaluated per fragment at real-time rates. Three modules realize it: a 2D Gaussian mixture anchored
in barycentric coordinates for the view-independent fields, an adaptive hierarchical environment
map for far-field and near-field reflection, and a FastKAN decoder for the view-dependent term.
Because the field is anchored to the faces, the same optimization can also refine the mesh it is
baked onto. With this bridge in place, the next steps are engine integration, evaluation on real
captures, and element-level operations such as merging or editing disks directly.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Our method requires a mesh, and is optimized per scene. We evaluate on
synthetic single-object benchmarks; real captures, where the input mesh is noisier and the
illumination less controlled, remain untested. Within a near-convex part, light arriving from the
rest of the object is treated as distant and position-independent, so strongly concave objects are
the hardest case: \emph{toaster} is where our margin is smallest. Real-time evaluation is
demonstrated in nvdiffrast rather than in a shipped fragment shader; the representation is
shader-implementable by construction, but engine integration is left to future work. Mesh refinement
adjusts an existing surface and cannot recover geometry that is absent, and is validated with a
single environment map.
