\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:Exp}
We evaluate on NeRF-Synthetic~\citep{NeRFVanilla} and Shiny-Blender~\citep{refnerf}. Two baselines
share our fixed-mesh setting: DNR~\citep{DNR}, and a mesh-constrained 2DGS, denoted
2DGS$^{*}$~\citep{2DGS}, in which four million 2D Gaussians are initialized uniformly on the surface
and all gradients are projected onto the manifold defined by the mesh faces. We also report
unconstrained methods (3DGS~\citep{3DGS}, Ref-NeRF~\citep{refnerf}, 3DGS-DR~\citep{3dgsDR}) and
flexible-mesh methods (GaussianShader~\citep{gaussianShader}, RefGS~\citep{refgs}) as reference;
these optimize geometry rather than taking it as given, so they are not directly comparable.


\subsection{Comparison}
Among fixed-mesh methods (Table~\ref{tab:main}) we attain the best PSNR and SSIM on both datasets
and the best LPIPS on Shiny-Blender, where we also exceed the unconstrained and flexible-mesh
results. Qualitatively (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative}), our renders reproduce sharp, high-frequency
environment reflections on metallic surfaces most faithfully; 2DGS$^{*}$ and DNR blur them and RefGS
recovers them less crisply.





\subsection{Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} follows the path by which the appearance model was built. The coplanar
2DGS$^{*}$ baseline suffers the depth-fighting of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, and a purely diffuse mixture
cannot represent view-dependent reflection, so both collapse on the reflective scenes. Per-primitive
view-dependent color --- spherical harmonics in 3DGS, or the anisotropic spherical Gaussians of
Spec-Gaussian~\citep{specGaussian} --- is strong for \emph{floating} splats, where overlapping,
depth-ordered primitives compose the appearance. On a single opaque surface that composition is
unavailable: each point takes one order-independent blend, so a per-primitive spherical Gaussian
with an MLP decoder only overfits the sparse views ($34.24$~dB), and dropping it barely changes the
result (Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}). The gains come instead from the adaptive hierarchical maps
($+1.2$~dB over a single global map) and from the KAN decoder ($+1.39$~dB over a parameter-matched
MLP).



\subsection{Efficiency and Downstream Tasks}
The full asset ($10^6$ disks, five environment maps, shader) occupies $48.3$ MB on disk and renders
the test views at $91.7$ FPS on NeRF-Synthetic and $68.6$ FPS on Shiny-Blender, measured with
\texttt{torch.compile} on a single NVIDIA H100 (CUDA 12.8). Table~\ref{tab:refinement} reports mesh
refinement: averaged over the six Shiny-Blender scenes, starting from Ref-GS marching-cubes
extractions, normal angular error falls by $14.2\%$ (vertex) and $18.3\%$ (geometric) and Chamfer
distance by $2.3\%$, while held-out PSNR improves by $0.45$~dB and LPIPS is unchanged. Per-scene
results, the algorithm, and implementation details are in App.~\ref{app:refinement}.
