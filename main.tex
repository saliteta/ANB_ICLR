\begin{abstract}
Baking precomputes an object's appearance into a texture on a mesh, but baked formats cannot
represent sharp view-dependent reflection. Neural and splat representations can, yet they place
appearance in the space \emph{around} the object rather than on its surface, so there is nothing to
bake; binding splats to mesh faces does not help, because coplanar primitives have tied depth keys
and no ordering of them is both view-stable and geometrically meaningful. We introduce Automatic
Neural Baking, which defines appearance as an \emph{on-manifold kernel field}: 2D Gaussian kernels
anchored to faces in barycentric coordinates, blended at each fragment by a partition of unity over
the $K$ nearest. A convex blend is invariant to the order of its terms, so the field needs no UV
atlas and no depth sort, and an $\mathcal{O}(K)$ local gather replaces the global
$\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ one. Two modules supply the view-dependent term: an adaptive environment map
split over near-convex parts, and a compact FastKAN decoder. Given the mesh, we reach $36.99$ dB
PSNR on Shiny-Blender, above the best reported results, and render at $76.8$ FPS on a consumer RTX
4090 with a $1.74$ GB footprint. Because the anchoring is linear in the vertices, appearance
gradients also reach the mesh: we derive how, and show they reduce the normal error of a coarse
extracted mesh on every scene.
\end{abstract}
